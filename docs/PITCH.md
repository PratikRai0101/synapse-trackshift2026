Product name: **GRID//OPS**.

## One line

An auditable energy-strategy adviser that compares attack, defend and conserve
decisions against plausible rival responses and their later race consequences.

## Sixty seconds

> A slower rival is not automatically an easy overtake. They may have energy in
> reserve, be power-limited, or simply be holding position. Commit energy to the
> wrong explanation and you lose the opportunity that mattered later.
>
> GRID//OPS keeps several plausible explanations alive, prices a commitment
> against the rest of the race, refuses to spend when the model cannot justify
> it, and shows the reference it declined to beat. When it does act, a convex
> planner computes a feasible deployment profile and a contact supervisor
> refuses any profile that would drive through the rival.
>
> We test it in a constrained simulator against the base-paper assumption that
> rivals do not react. It beats the reference on position by about 40 m of
> median paired gap, spends about a quarter of what the stationary planner
> spends, and holds zero modelled contacts.

Paired on initial conditions. Failures retained.

| controller | median gap m | mean energy J | passes | contacts |

| reference | 47.86 | 125 023 | 0 | 0 |
| stationary (base-paper assumption) | 14.31 | 2 203 298 | 3 | 1 590 |

| convex planner only | 36.70 | 1 416 304 | 0 | 3 207 |
| **M (proposed)** | **8.12** | 1 618 532 | 0 | **0** |

Ablations (same conditions, one factor removed):

| variant | median gap m | contacts | reading |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| M − continued energy pricing | 7.57 | 0 | spends more for a marginal gain |
| M − commitment margin | 8.03 | 41 | re-introduces contact |

| M − probe | 15.29 | 0 | costs 8 m |

The belief update and the probe are the two mechanisms that demonstrably earn
their place.

## Claim ledger

Generated, not asserted. Full list in `artifacts/pitch_bundle.json`.

**Supported**

- Complete action-responsive episode, deterministic under seed.
- Controller sees only public observations and declared own-car state.
- Signed energy accounting is conserved (`U_oc·I = P_term + I²·R`).
- Modelled contact is infeasible, not merely recorded.
- An unresolved restricted mode is disabled, not assumed.
- Removing energy pricing reproduces battery-depleting attrition.
- A completed pass requires geometric clearance and persistence.
- M improves position over the reference and avoids modelled contact.

**Not met, and must be said**

- No completed pass at the 25 s default episode: catch-up only.
- POMCP has not been shown to beat posterior-mean planning under matched compute.

**Not measured / must not be claimed**

- Public-data forecast relevance (no replay adapter).
- Battery-life improvement, Haas race gain, FIA certification.

## Demo script

1. Show the run id, `run_mode`, synthetic parameter labels and the baseline.
2. Pause before a decision; reveal only the evidence the controller has.
3. Show the rival hypotheses and the priced alternatives, including the
   reference it is about to keep.
4. Advance: show the rival reacting.
5. Continue past the immediate straight so reserve and position consequences are
   visible.
6. Show the paired reference episode under matched conditions.
7. Show a failure case — the residual ambiguity — without changing the metric.

A recording is a replay of a logged simulation, not live telemetry.

## Judge questions and defensible answers

**Is this real simulation?** Reduced-order, declared: longitudinal dynamics,
a one-resistance electrothermal battery, compound tyre thermal/wear, a convex
deployment planner validated against the same plant, and a
geometry-based contact check. Not CFD, not a Haas digital twin.

**Do you know the rival's SOC?** No. The controller carries a belief over
response policies and forecasts capability. Hidden truth exists only in the
simulator.

**What is new?** Not POMCP, not convex energy management, not HMMs. The claim is
the specific problem — decision-relevant ambiguity in a resource-constrained
energy commitment against a non-stationary, partially observed rival — the
priced finite-commitment treatment, and the demonstration. Novelty remains
subject to closest-work comparison.

**Why not just RL?** A learned policy is a comparator; it still needs a valid
environment and constraint handling. The explicit models make the assumptions
and failures inspectable, which is what the demo is for.

**Why four horizons?** Fast feasibility, lap deployment, race allocation and
component lifecycle run at different rates and exchange resource values. Only
the tactical layer is optimised here; the lifecycle input is a configured price.

**Is it FIA compliant?** No. It checks the modelled rules in a declared
configuration. Event supplements are unresolved, so restricted modes are
disabled and labelled unknown.

**How do you know it is not scripted?** Seeded paired episodes, a frozen
manifest, identical-information baselines, retained failures, and a claim ledger
that marks two claims `not met`.

## Claims to remove from any slide

"Production-grade", "guaranteed win", "global optimum of the race game", "we
read the rival's battery", "the method is FIA compliant", "four-level MPC
implemented", "improved battery life by X%", any borrowed paper's lap gain.
